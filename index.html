Nami is a house
for slowness.

Twelve saunas, four waters and one long exhale, two thousand metres above the sea. We keep the lights low, the voices lower, and the phones outside. What happens here is simple — heat, cold, rest, repeat — until the body remembers itself.

— ნამი, dew. The water that arrives without a sound.

900 candles, lit at dusk.

Warm walnut, black river basalt, unlacquered brass

Nami stands where the Adjarian road stops climbing, on the Goderdzi Pass. The shell was a 1930s weather station in stone and larch; we kept the walls and let everything else go quiet: oiled walnut, basalt lifted from the Ajaristsqali riverbed, linen the colour of wet sand, brass that is allowed to age.

Nothing here is polished to a shine. Warmth is the only decoration — a strip of light behind a screen, a fire that is never fully out, steam that finds the ceiling and comes back down as rain. Outside, weather that changes its mind twice an hour.

Good to know

Every ticket includes a linen robe, two towels and slippers. The sauna world is textile-free and adults only — 18+, without exception. Bring nothing; lockers, water and tea are on the house. Phones stay in the locker, and we mean it kindly. It is a two-and-a-half-hour drive from the coast, so most guests take the day — we hold a car for four at Batumi Boulevard at 09:00 and 13:00, and drive you back down after dinner.
